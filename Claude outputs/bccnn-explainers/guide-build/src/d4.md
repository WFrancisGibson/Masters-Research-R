::: abstract
By default the number of gradient-descent steps of the blended cross-classified neural network is not chosen on a split of the individual claims into two portfolios, but on a split of the observed claims triangle along calendar time. This report explains that time-aware split. The valuation date is moved back twice, the network is fitted on what would have been known at each earlier date, and it is scored on the calendar periods that followed; a third partition, the whole observed triangle, supplies the number of steps of the reported fit. In every partition the latest calendar periods, apart from a border of early accident and development periods, stop the training.

The report defines the partitions cell by cell, derives closed forms for their sizes, and proves the properties that the code relies on: every accident period and every development period keeps a training cell, so that the chain-ladder start can be fitted on the training cells; the latest accident and development periods stay in training; and no cell of the true lower triangle enters any partition. It then follows the fits that are run on the partitions: the early-stopping run on each partition, the out-of-time scoring of the two test partitions, the pooled test error, and the transfer of the chosen number of steps to the final fit.

The report closes with a comparison of the validation signal of this split with that of the claims split, a map of each step to the code, and a replication of the split in Python that confirms the cell counts and the properties for the configuration of the code.

# INTRODUCTION {#d4-ch1}

## Introduction {#d4-s1}

This report is one of four explanatory texts on the blended cross-classified neural network (bCCNN), a network that starts from the cross-classified over-dispersed Poisson (ccODP) model, as implemented in the code of the repository. The four texts are referred to as companions. Companion I is *The mathematics behind the bCCNN: the ccODP model, the network and its chain-ladder start*; companion II is *The fitting procedure and training of the bCCNN*; companion III is *The bCCNN as a whole: interpretation, evaluation and uncertainty*; and companion IV is the present report, *The time-aware split for the bCCNN: rolling-origin training, validation and test partitions*.

The configuration file `config.yml` sets `validation: rolling_origin`, so by default the number of gradient-descent steps of the bCCNN, taken with the root mean square propagation (RMSprop) optimiser, is not chosen on the split of the claims used by Gabrielli, Richman and Wüthrich (2020), referred to as Paper C as in the code, but on a split of the observed triangle along calendar time, after Al-Mudafer, Avanzi, Taylor and Wong (2021). The report explains `rolling_origin_sets()` in `R/triangles.R` and `bccnn_ro_partition()`, `bccnn_rolling_origin()` and `bccnn_calibrate()` in `R/nn_models.R`, read against the description of the partitions and of the training in Al-Mudafer *et al.* (2021: 10, 16).

The question addressed is how the observed triangle is cut into training, validation and test cells along calendar time, what this cut guarantees for the fits of the ccODP model and of the network that are run on it, and what the resulting validation signal measures. To answer it, the report defines the split cell by cell, proves the properties the code relies on, counts the cells for the configuration of the code, and follows the three fits that are run on it: the early-stopping run on each partition, the out-of-time scoring of the test partitions, and the transfer of the chosen number of steps to the final fit. It ends with a comparison of what this split measures against what the claims split measures.

## Why a split along time {#d4-s2}

The quantity the bCCNN predicts is the lower triangle: the payments of the calendar periods *after* the valuation date. A model is good if what it learned on calendar periods $\le n$ carries over to calendar periods $> n$. Three ways of building a validation signal differ in how closely they imitate that question.

*A random hold-out of cells.* A random hold-out of cells of the same triangle measures interpolation between neighbouring cells. On a $20 \times 20$ triangle it is not even feasible for the ccODP start, which needs a cell in every accident and development period, and it is what Paper C ruled out for its 78-cell triangles (Gabrielli, Richman and Wüthrich, 2018: 11).

*The 50/50 split of the claims.* The split of the claims into two halves (Paper C; companion II, Section ⟦ref:d2-s3⟧) measures how well the surface learned on one half of the portfolio fits an independent copy of the *same* upper triangle: the same accident periods, development periods and calendar periods. It is an out-of-sample signal, but not an out-of-time one.

*The rolling origin.* The rolling origin moves the valuation date back, fits on what would have been known then, and scores on the calendar periods that followed. This is the standard device of forecast evaluation, the 'rolling forecasting origin' (Tashman, 2000), described as time series cross-validation by Hyndman and Athanasopoulos (2021), and of reserving back-tests (removing the latest diagonals). Al-Mudafer *et al.* apply it to a $40 \times 40$ quarterly triangle: "In the first partition, the data is assumed to comprise a 30×30 triangle, which leaves the latest 10 calendar periods for the testing set. [...] The second partition works with a 36×36 triangle, leaving 4 calendar periods for testing" (Al-Mudafer *et al.*, 2021: 10), and the third partition is the whole triangle, on which the chosen model is trained. The two test partitions deliberately probe different horizons: the first "tests long-term projection", the second "recent trends" (comment in `R/triangles.R`).

Al-Mudafer *et al.* fit a mixture density network (MDN) to a triangle indexed by accident quarters (AQ) and development quarters (DQ). In all three partitions the validation set, which stops the training, is also chosen by time: "the validation set included the 4 latest non-testing calendar periods, excluding the first 3 accident and development periods", so that "when combined with Early Stopping [...], the MDN stops training when short term projection accuracy is maximised" (Al-Mudafer *et al.*, 2021: 10). The code scales these numbers from $40 \times 40$ quarters to $20 \times 20$ years: test horizons $(5, 2)$ for their $(10, 4)$, $v = 2$ validation diagonals for their 4, and $e = 2$ excluded border periods for their 3.

# CONSTRUCTION OF THE PARTITIONS {#d4-ch2}

## Introduction {#d4-ch2-intro}

Chapter ⟦ref:d4-ch1⟧ argued that the validation signal should imitate the forecast of later calendar periods. This chapter turns that argument into a construction. It first defines the triangle that would have been observed at an earlier valuation date and the test cells that follow it, then the three partitions built by `rolling_origin_sets()`, and finally the properties of the split that the fits of Chapter ⟦ref:d4-ch3⟧ rely on. To avoid a clash with the ccODP intercept $c$, the valuation period of a partition (`origin`, `c0` in the code) is written $\tau$.

## Calendar periods and the triangle observed at an earlier date {#d4-s3}

Cell $(i, j)$ belongs to calendar period $k = i + j - 1$; the cells of one calendar period form a diagonal of the square. At the valuation date $n$ the observed triangle is $\mathcal{D} = \{(i, j) : k \le n\}$. Figure ⟦ref:d4-fig1⟧ shows the calendar period $k = i + j - 1$ of each cell of a $7 \times 7$ square; moving the valuation date back from 7 to $\tau$ removes the diagonals $k > \tau$.

![](figures/calendar_diagonals.png){width=8.6cm}

⟦figcap:d4-fig1|Calendar period of each cell of a 7 × 7 square⟧

A reserving actuary working at the earlier valuation date $\tau < n$ would have seen accident periods $1, \dots, \tau$, development periods $1, \dots, \tau$, and only the cells with $k \le \tau$:

$$
\mathcal{D}_\tau = \{(i, j) : 1 \le i, j \le \tau, \ i + j - 1 \le \tau\}, \qquad |\mathcal{D}_\tau| = \frac{\tau (\tau + 1)}{2} .
$$
⟦eq:d4-eq1⟧

Any model fitted on $\mathcal{D}_\tau$ can forecast only cells of the $\tau \times \tau$ square: the ccODP has effects for the periods $\le \tau$ only, and so has the bCCNN, whose embeddings have $\tau$ categories. Of those cells, the ones that lie after $\tau$ but were observed by $n$ are the test cells of the partition,

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal{T}_\tau &= \{(i, j) : 1 \le i, j \le \tau, \ \tau < i + j - 1 \le n\}, \\
|\mathcal{T}_\tau| &= \sum_{k = \tau + 1}^{n} (2\tau - k) \qquad (n \le 2\tau - 1),
\end{aligned}
$$
⟦eq:d4-eq2⟧

because diagonal $k \ge \tau$ of a $\tau \times \tau$ square holds the cells $i = k + 1 - \tau, \dots, \tau$, that is $2\tau - k$ of them. For $\tau = 15$: $14 + 13 + 12 + 11 + 10 = 60$ cells; for $\tau = 18$: $17 + 16 = 33$. The cells of $\mathcal{D}$ outside the $\tau \times \tau$ square (accident periods $> \tau$, or development periods $> \tau$) are observed but unused by that partition, and the true lower triangle $\mathcal{F}$ is used by no partition at all.

## The partitions {#d4-s4}

`rolling_origin_sets(m, test_periods = c(5, 2), vali_periods = 2, exclude = 2)` first cuts its input to the observed triangle, `y <- upper(m)`, so nothing below the diagonal $k = n$ can enter. It then builds one partition per valuation period

$$
\tau \in \{ n - t_1, \ n - t_2, \ n \} = \{15, 18, 20\},
$$
⟦eq:d4-eq3⟧

test partitions first, the final one last. For each $\tau$ it takes the $\tau \times \tau$ square, computes $k$ for every cell, and defines the validation, training and test cells as follows.

*Validation cells.* The validation cells are the latest $v$ diagonals of $\mathcal{D}_\tau$, without the first $e$ accident periods and the first $e$ development periods,

$$
\mathcal{V}^{\mathrm{diag}}_\tau = \{(i, j) \in \mathcal{D}_\tau : \ k > \tau - v, \ i > e, \ j > e\},
$$
⟦eq:d4-eq4⟧

plus $K = v (e - 1)$ replacement cells at the excluded early development periods (Al-Mudafer *et al.* (2021), as quoted in the comments of `R/triangles.R`: the "DQ2 and DQ3 validation points ... taken evenly from earlier AQs"). With $lo = e + 1$ and $hi = \tau - v - e + 1$, the replacement cells sit at development periods $e, e - 1, \dots, 2$ (cycled $K$ times; with $e = 2$ both at development period 2) and at the accident periods at the centres of $K$ equal bins of $[lo, hi]$,

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal{E}_\tau &= \{ (r_m, d_m) : m = 1, \dots, K \}, \\
r_m &= \operatorname{round}\Big( lo + (hi - lo) \frac{m - \tfrac12}{K} \Big), \qquad d_m = e - \big( (m - 1) \bmod (e - 1) \big), \\
\mathcal{V}_\tau &= \mathcal{V}^{\mathrm{diag}}_\tau \cup \mathcal{E}_\tau .
\end{aligned}
$$
⟦eq:d4-eq5⟧

R's `round()` rounds halves to even, as the Python replication does. For the configuration of the code $K = 2$, $lo = 3$, and $hi = 12, 15, 17$ for $\tau = 15, 18, 20$, giving the replacement cells $(5, 2), (10, 2)$; $(6, 2), (12, 2)$; $(6, 2), (14, 2)$.

*Training cells.* The training cells are everything observed at $\tau$ that is not validation: $\mathcal{U}_\tau = \mathcal{D}_\tau \setminus \mathcal{V}_\tau$.

*Test cells.* The test cells are $\mathcal{T}_\tau$ of ⟦ref:d4-eq2⟧ for the test partitions; the final partition ($\tau = n$) has none.

Each partition is returned as a list with the $\tau \times \tau$ matrix of payments observed at $\tau$ (`y`, `NA` after $\tau$), the logical masks `train` and `vali`, the matrix `test` (payments on $\mathcal{T}_\tau$, `NA` elsewhere, `NULL` for the final partition), a `role` matrix for the plots, `origin` $= \tau$, `final`, and `n`.

Figure ⟦ref:d4-fig2⟧ shows the three partitions of the $20 \times 20$ triangle for `test_periods = c(5, 2)`, `vali_periods = 2` and `exclude = 2`. The training cells are light green. The validation cells are dark green: the last two diagonals of each partition minus the first two accident and development periods, plus two cells at development period 2. The test cells are red: the calendar years after $\tau$ up to 20, inside the $\tau \times \tau$ square. Hatched cells were observed at year 20 but lie outside the partition's square; grey cells form the true lower triangle, used by no partition.

## Properties of the split {#d4-s5}

The construction of Section ⟦ref:d4-s4⟧ has seven properties, labelled (a) to (g), on which the code and the fits of Chapter ⟦ref:d4-ch3⟧ rely.

*(a) Every accident period and every development period of $\mathcal{D}_\tau$ has a training cell.* Accident periods $i \le e$: none of their cells is on a validation diagonal ⟦ref:d4-eq4⟧, and the replacement rows satisfy $r_m \ge lo = e + 1$, so the whole row is training. Accident periods $i > e$: the cell $(i, 1)$ has $j = 1 \le e$, so it is excluded from ⟦ref:d4-eq4⟧, and the replacement cells have $d_m \ge 2$, so $(i, 1)$ is training. Development periods $j \le e$: the cell $(1, j)$ is in accident period $1 \le e$, hence training. Development periods $j > e$: the cell $(1, j)$ is in accident period 1, hence training. In short, the first $e$ rows and the first column are entirely training, and that is enough. This is the guarantee `fit_odp_glm(cells = part$train)` needs ("every accident and development period needs at least one cell to fit on") and the reason the comment in `R/triangles.R` says the exclusions make the ccODP start fittable on the training cells. The Python replication confirms it for all three partitions.

*(b) Counts.* Diagonal $k \le \tau$ of $\mathcal{D}_\tau$ has $k$ cells, $i = 1, \dots, k$; removing $i \le e$ and $j = k + 1 - i \le e$ removes $2e$ of them. Hence

$$
\begin{aligned}
|\mathcal{V}_\tau| &= \sum_{k = \tau - v + 1}^{\tau} (k - 2e) + v (e - 1) \\
&= v (\tau - 2e) - \frac{v (v - 1)}{2} + v (e - 1), \\
|\mathcal{U}_\tau| &= \frac{\tau (\tau + 1)}{2} - |\mathcal{V}_\tau| ,
\end{aligned}
$$
⟦eq:d4-eq6⟧

which gives $23, 29, 33$ validation cells and $97, 142, 177$ training cells for $\tau = 15, 18, 20$, as in Table ⟦ref:d4-tab1⟧. The validation share of the observed cells is $19.2\%$, $17.0\%$, $15.7\%$.

*(c) The latest accident periods and the latest development periods stay in training.* Accident period $\tau$ has the single cell $(\tau, 1)$, and accident period $\tau - 1$ the cells $(\tau - 1, 1), (\tau - 1, 2)$: all have $j \le e$ and are training. Likewise development periods $\tau$ and $\tau - 1$ consist of $(1, \tau)$ and $(1, \tau - 1), (2, \tau - 1)$, all in accident periods $\le e$, all training. So the network keeps learning the level of the most recent accident periods and the tail of the development pattern, while being stopped on the most recent diagonals of the periods in between. This is the point of the exclusion of Al-Mudafer *et al.*: without it, the latest accident period would have no training cell at all (its only cell is on the last diagonal), and a cross-classified model could not even be started.

*(d) The replacement cells lie off the validation diagonals.* A replacement cell has calendar period $r_m + d_m - 1 \le hi + e - 1 = \tau - v$, so it is earlier than every diagonal of ⟦ref:d4-eq4⟧: it adds validation information about the early development periods (which ⟦ref:d4-eq4⟧ removed) without touching the latest diagonals, and it is spread over the accident periods $[lo, hi]$ so as not to cluster. For the final partition the two cells are $(6, 2)$ and $(14, 2)$, calendar periods 7 and 15.

*(e) No leakage.* The input is cut to $\mathcal{D}$ before anything else, so the true lower triangle is in no partition. Within a partition, fitting and early stopping read only cells with $k \le \tau$; the cells with $\tau < k \le n$ are read by the scoring alone (Section ⟦ref:d4-s8⟧), and only for the test partitions. The final partition's run tracks the true lower triangle (`truth`) for a plot, after the fact, without using it.

*(f) The partitions are nested in time, not in cells.* $\mathcal{D}_{15} \subset \mathcal{D}_{18} \subset \mathcal{D}_{20} = \mathcal{D}$, but the validation sets are not nested: the last two diagonals of the $15 \times 15$ triangle are training cells of the $18 \times 18$ one. Each partition is a complete, self-contained imitation of the reserving problem at its own valuation date.

*(g) Scaling.* The values $(10, 4) / 4 / 3$ of Al-Mudafer *et al.* on $40 \times 40$ quarters hold out $25\%$ and $10\%$ of the calendar periods for testing and $10\%$ for validation; the values $(5, 2) / 2 / 2$ of the code on $20 \times 20$ years keep the same proportions of calendar periods ($25\%$, $10\%$, $10\%$) and shrink the border from three periods to two. The function stops if a partition would be too small for the requested validation and border periods ($\tau - v < 2e + 1$).

## Summary {#d4-ch2-summary}

The rolling origin cuts the observed triangle into three partitions with valuation periods 15, 18 and 20. Each partition consists of the triangle that would have been observed at its valuation date; its latest two diagonals, without the first two accident and development periods, plus two replacement cells at development period 2, are the validation cells, and the rest are the training cells. The two test partitions are scored on the 60 and 33 cells observed after their valuation dates inside their squares. The construction guarantees a training cell in every accident and development period, keeps the latest accident and development periods in training, and keeps the true lower triangle out of every partition. Chapter ⟦ref:d4-ch3⟧ follows the fits that are run on these partitions.

# FITTING AND SCORING ON THE PARTITIONS {#d4-ch3}

## Introduction {#d4-ch3-intro}

Chapter ⟦ref:d4-ch2⟧ constructed the partitions and their properties. This chapter follows what the code does with them: the ccODP start fitted on the training cells of each partition, the early-stopping run on each partition, the out-of-time scoring of the test partitions, the pooling of their scores into one test error per model, and the transfer of the number of steps chosen on the final partition to the reported model.

## The ccODP start on the training cells {#d4-s6}

For each partition `bccnn_ro_partition()` fits the ccODP on the training cells only, `fit_odp_glm(part$y, cells = part$train)`, giving $\hat\vartheta(\mathcal{U}_\tau) = (\hat c, \hat\alpha_1, \dots, \hat\alpha_\tau, \hat\beta_1, \dots, \hat\beta_\tau)$ with $2\tau - 1$ parameters. The maximum likelihood equations ⟦ref:d1-eq12⟧ of companion I then hold over the training cells: the fitted row and column totals over $\mathcal{U}_\tau$ equal the observed ones over $\mathcal{U}_\tau$. This has two consequences.

*The start is not the chain ladder of the partition.* The start of the partition's network is *not* the chain ladder of $\mathcal{D}_\tau$, which would use all of $\mathcal{D}_\tau$ including the validation diagonals. It is the ccODP of an irregular set of cells. The chain ladder at $\tau$, $\hat\vartheta(\mathcal{D}_\tau)$, is fitted separately for the scoring (`cl <- fit_odp_glm(part$y)`), so that the test partitions report two chain-ladder-type baselines: the chain ladder as it would have been reserved at $\tau$ (`test_ccODP`), and the training-cell ccODP that the network started from (`test_ccODP_train`, which is also the `test_pred` of the partition's history at step 0).

*The gradient at the start.* Property 2 of companion I (Section ⟦ref:d1-s6.4⟧: zero gradient in $c$ and $w$ at the start, only $B$ moves at step 1) holds on the training cells, because it only needs the marginal-total equations on the cells the loss is computed on.

Unlike the claims split, the training cells carry the full volume of the portfolio, so $\hat c$ is on the scale of the whole triangle; what is removed is cells, not claims.

## The early-stopping run on a partition {#d4-s7}

Each partition then receives the run of companion II (Section ⟦ref:d2-s7⟧) with its own data: from $\theta_0(\hat\vartheta(\mathcal{U}_\tau))$, $T_{\max} = 1 000$ full-batch RMSprop steps on the $|\mathcal{U}_\tau|$ training cells with dropout, recording after every step the deviance without dropout on the training cells, on the validation cells,

$$
D_{V,\tau}(t) = D\big( \boldsymbol y, \ \boldsymbol\mu(\theta_t); \ \mathcal{V}_\tau \big), \qquad t = 0, \dots, T_{\max},
$$
⟦eq:d4-eq7⟧

on the test cells $\mathcal{T}_\tau$ (test partitions; column `test`), and on the true lower triangle (final partition; column `truth`), together with the predicted sums over each of those sets (`*_pred`). The step with the lowest validation deviance,

$$
t^*_\tau = \arg\min_{0 \le t \le T_{\max}} D_{V,\tau}(t),
$$
⟦eq:d4-eq8⟧

the decreases `loss_decrease(h, best)` and the ccODP are kept. Because the partition's batch is its $|\mathcal{U}_\tau|$ training cells, each partition is a separate Keras model built with the same seed: the same initial hidden-layer weights, but, since the batch sizes differ, different dropout mask sequences. For the final partition the whole path of mean squares is kept (`keep_mu = TRUE` when `final_fit = "partition"`), so that its early-stopped network can be reported without retraining (Section ⟦ref:d4-s10⟧).

*Periods without payments.* Property (a) guarantees each period a training *cell*, not a training *payment*. If all training cells of a development period hold zero, for instance a late development period whose only payment of the partition sits on a validation diagonal, the ccODP on the training cells gives that period an effect of about $-30$ and fitted means of order $e^{-30}$ (companion I, Section ⟦ref:d1-s3.9⟧). A validation cell with a positive payment there would add about $30\,y$ to $D_{V,\tau}(t)$ at every step, and could decide $t^*_\tau$ by itself. `bccnn_ro_partition()` therefore applies `mask_zero_periods()` with the training-cell ccODP: those validation cells are left out of ⟦ref:d4-eq7⟧, as they are under the claims split, and $\mathcal{V}_\tau$ in ⟦ref:d4-eq7⟧ and ⟦ref:d4-eq8⟧ means the validation cells actually scored. Neither Al-Mudafer *et al.* nor Paper C nor Härkönen (2021) discusses this case.

## Scoring the test partitions out of time {#d4-s8}

A test partition answers the following question: had the procedure been applied at valuation date $\tau$, how well would it have forecast the next $n - \tau$ calendar periods? `bccnn_ro_partition()` scores two things on $\mathcal{T}_\tau$, with `final_fit` deciding which network stands for the procedure.

*Refit.* With `final_fit = "refit"` (the default, the final fit of Paper C), the procedure *as it is reported* is replayed at $\tau$. The chain ladder of $\mathcal{D}_\tau$ gives the start, and the network is trained on all of $\mathcal{D}_\tau$ for exactly $t^*_\tau$ steps,

$$
\begin{aligned}
\theta^{\mathrm{refit}}_\tau &= \mathcal{T}^{s}_{t^*_\tau}\big( \theta_0(\hat\vartheta(\mathcal{D}_\tau)), \ \mathcal{D}_\tau \big), \\
\text{test loss} &= D\big( \boldsymbol y, \ \boldsymbol\mu(\theta^{\mathrm{refit}}_\tau); \ \mathcal{T}_\tau \big), \\
\text{test prediction} &= \sum_{\mathcal{T}_\tau} \mu_{i,j}(\theta^{\mathrm{refit}}_\tau) .
\end{aligned}
$$
⟦eq:d4-eq9⟧

This is a genuine out-of-time back-test of the whole chain "choose $t^*$ on the latest diagonals, refit on everything, forecast": every number in ⟦ref:d4-eq9⟧ could have been produced at $\tau$.

*Partition.* With `final_fit = "partition"` (Al-Mudafer *et al.*), the early-stopped network of the partition itself, $\theta_{t^*_\tau}$ trained on $\mathcal{U}_\tau$, is scored: the test loss and test prediction are read from the history at step $t^*_\tau$.

Test cells receive the same treatment as validation cells, with a different reference. A test cell is left out if its accident or development period has no payments in *any* cell observed at $\tau$, that is, if the chain ladder at $\tau$ flags it: no model fitted at $\tau$ could forecast it. A test cell in a period that has payments only in the validation cells stays in. The chain ladder at $\tau$ does know that period, so a variant that loses it, such as the `partition` network started from the training-cell ccODP, is scored for the loss. All three models are scored on the same test cells, and `n_test` counts them.

Next to the bCCNN, the partition records the actual test sum $\sum_{\mathcal{T}_\tau} y_{i,j}$, the chain ladder at $\tau$ (its test sum and test deviance) and the training-cell ccODP (its test sum and test deviance from the history at step 0). The comment in the code names which baseline is like-for-like with which variant: the chain ladder at $\tau$ for `refit`, the training-cell ccODP for `partition`.

## The pooled test error {#d4-s9}

`bccnn_rolling_origin()` pools the test partitions into one number per model, the test deviance per test cell of equation (4.4) of Al-Mudafer *et al.*,

$$
\begin{aligned}
\bar D^{M} &= \frac{ \sum_{\tau \in \{15, 18\}} D\big( \boldsymbol y, \boldsymbol\mu^{M}_\tau; \mathcal{T}_\tau \big) }{ \sum_{\tau \in \{15, 18\}} |\mathcal{T}_\tau| }, \\
M &\in \{\text{ccODP fitted at } \tau, \ \text{ccODP on } \mathcal{U}_\tau, \ \text{bCCNN}\},
\end{aligned}
$$
⟦eq:d4-eq10⟧

with $60 + 33 = 93$ test cells in the denominator (fewer if a test partition has a period without payments by $\tau$, Section ⟦ref:d4-s8⟧). It is cell-weighted, so the long-horizon partition ($\tau = 15$, five calendar years ahead) weighs almost twice the short-horizon one; equation (4.4) of Al-Mudafer *et al.* averages the negative log-likelihood of their density forecasts in the same way, and the code uses the deviance because the bCCNN is a mean model. `bccnn_tables()` prints the per-partition losses, their per-cell versions, and the pooled row labelled "test error (4.4)".

*What the test error is for.* In Al-Mudafer *et al.* the test error is the objective of the hyperparameter search ("The MDN's architecture was selected using an algorithm that successively optimised one hyper-parameter at a time" (Al-Mudafer *et al.*, 2021: 11)), averaged over several seeds to damp the initialisation (Al-Mudafer *et al.*, 2021: 16). In the code the architecture is fixed by `config.yml`, so $\bar D$ is *reported*, not optimised: it shows whether the bCCNN procedure beat the chain ladder out of time at two earlier valuation dates, in the same units as the in-sample and out-of-sample deviances of the final fit, before the true lower triangle is looked at. It is a single realisation per seed (the comment in `bccnn_rolling_origin()`: "results depend on the seed"); comparing designs by it would need the seed averaging that Al-Mudafer *et al.* perform.

## From the final partition to the reported model {#d4-s10}

The number of steps of the final fit is that of the final partition,

$$
\begin{aligned}
t^* &= t^*_{n}, \qquad \text{chosen on } \mathcal{V}_{20}: \\
&\text{the diagonals } k = 19, 20 \text{ minus the border, plus } (6, 2), (14, 2) .
\end{aligned}
$$
⟦eq:d4-eq11⟧

`bccnn_calibrate()` then proceeds exactly as under the claims split (companion II, Sections ⟦ref:d2-s8⟧ and ⟦ref:d2-s9⟧): under `refit`, the chain ladder of $\mathcal{D}$ is fitted and the network is trained on all 210 cells for $t^*$ steps from it, $\hat\theta = \mathcal{T}^{s}_{t^*}(\theta_0(\hat\vartheta(\mathcal{D})), \mathcal{D})$; under `partition`, the mean square of the final partition's network at step $t^*$ is taken from the stored path, with no second training. The decreases of Paper C's Table 3 and the bCCNN dispersion $\hat\phi^{\mathrm{bCCNN}} = \hat\phi_D (1 - \max(0, \Delta_V))$ are computed from the final partition's curve, so $\Delta_V$ is now the relative decrease of the deviance on the latest two diagonals rather than on an independent half of the portfolio.

The transfer of $t^*$ rests on the same assumption as under the claims split, that the loss surface of the full triangle is close to the one the run explored, and it is a milder assumption here in one respect and a stronger one in another. It is milder because the training cells of the final partition are 177 of the 210 cells of the same portfolio, with the same intercept scale, rather than half the claims. It is stronger because the 33 cells that the refit adds are the latest diagonals, the cells nearest to the forecast region, and the refit trains on them for $t^*$ steps without any check on them.

## Summary {#d4-ch3-summary}

On each partition the ccODP is fitted on the training cells only, which keeps the marginal-total equations and hence the behaviour of the network at the start, but makes the start differ from the chain ladder at the valuation date. The early-stopping run records the validation deviance after every step and chooses the step with the lowest value; validation and test cells in periods without payments are masked so that a single cell cannot decide the result. The two test partitions score the procedure out of time, either as a refit at the earlier valuation date or as the early-stopped network of the partition, and their deviances are pooled into a cell-weighted test error per model, which the code reports rather than optimises. The number of steps of the reported model is the one chosen on the final partition. Chapter ⟦ref:d4-ch4⟧ compares this validation signal with the claims split and verifies the construction numerically.

## Introduction {#d4-ch4-intro}

Chapters ⟦ref:d4-ch2⟧ and ⟦ref:d4-ch3⟧ described the rolling origin on its own. This chapter places it next to the claims split of Paper C and Härkönen (2021), maps each step of the construction and of the fits to the code, and reports the Python replication that checks the cell counts and properties of Chapter ⟦ref:d4-ch2⟧.

## What the two validation signals measure {#d4-s11}

Table ⟦ref:d4-tab2⟧ sets the two early-stopping signals available in `config.yml` side by side.

⟦tabcap:d4-tab2|The two early-stopping signals available in `config.yml`⟧

| Aspect | Claims split (Paper C, Härkönen) | Rolling origin (Al-Mudafer *et al.*) |
|:--|:--|:--|
| What is split | The individual claims, into two portfolios | The cells of the observed triangle, by calendar period |
| Training data of the run | The training half's upper triangle, 210 cells, half the volume | The training cells $\mathcal{U}_\tau$: 177 of 210 cells (final partition), full volume |
| Validation data | The validation half's upper triangle, 210 cells (minus zero periods): the same $(i, j)$ pairs as training | The latest two diagonals minus the border, plus two early cells: 33 cells, $(i, j)$ pairs the run never trained on |
| What $D_V$ estimates | Out-of-sample fit at the same accident, development and calendar periods: interpolation in an independent copy | Out-of-sample fit one and two calendar periods ahead of the training cells: short-term extrapolation |
| Start of the run | ccODP of the training half (intercept lower by about $\log 2$) | ccODP of the training cells (not the chain ladder of the partition) |
| Out-of-time check before the truth is used | None | The test error ⟦ref:d4-eq10⟧ at two earlier valuation dates |
| Randomness in the split | The allocation of the claims (results change when the halves are swapped; Härkönen, 2021: 33-34) | None: the split is deterministic given the configuration |
| What is transferred to the final fit | $t^*$ | $t^*$ of the final partition (or the partition's network itself) |
| Cost | One ccODP, one run | Three ccODPs and three runs, plus two refits for the scoring |

Neither signal measures what the back-test measures, the deviance on the true lower triangle, which is 190 cells up to 19 calendar periods ahead; test partition 1 of the rolling origin (five periods ahead) is the closest a procedure can come without looking at the truth. For a short-tailed annual triangle, where most of the reserve sits in the first few development periods of the latest accident periods, 'short-term projection accuracy' is also most of the reserve, which is the case for stopping on the latest diagonals.

## Summary and conclusions {#d4-ch4-summary}

The rolling origin replaces the claims split as the default early-stopping signal of the bCCNN. Where the claims split measures interpolation in an independent copy of the same upper triangle, the rolling origin stops the training on the one and two calendar periods that follow the training cells, and scores the whole procedure out of time at two earlier valuation dates before the true lower triangle is used. The split is deterministic given the configuration, and it costs three ccODP fits and three runs, plus two refits for the scoring.

The construction in `rolling_origin_sets()` follows Al-Mudafer *et al.* (2021) with their proportions of calendar periods scaled from quarters to years. Its exclusion of the first accident and development periods from the validation diagonals guarantees that every period keeps a training cell, so that the ccODP start can be fitted on the training cells, and its replacement cells restore validation information at the early development periods without touching the latest diagonals. The Python replication confirms the cell counts, the replacement cells and these guarantees for the configuration of the code.

Two limitations remain. The transfer of the number of steps to the final fit trains on the latest diagonals without any check on them, and the pooled test error is a single realisation per seed, so comparing designs by it would require averaging over seeds as Al-Mudafer *et al.* do. Neither validation signal measures the deviance on the true lower triangle; the long-horizon test partition is the closest a procedure can come without looking at the truth.
